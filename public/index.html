The information hierarchy

What the deck establishes, in order. Tap any layer.

Foundation
It's a mountain bike that also crushes gravel & bikepacking.
+
Not a gravel bike that dabbles in trails — an all-terrain bike built on MTB geometry. Long reach, short stem, wide bars, low BB. Reviewers consistently say it "rides like a modern hardtail." That's the thing that makes it fun, and fun is what a drop-bar bike can't fake.
Layer 02 · The math
One bike replaces two or three in the lineup.
+
For the customer: one maintenance rhythm, one parts family, one machine instead of a quiver. For the dealer: a single premium SKU absorbs the work of several margin-diluting entry-to-mid bikes.
Layer 03 · The people
Three customer stories: joy, simplification, access.
+
The same frame sells three different ways depending on who's standing in the shop. The deck arms staff to recognize which story they're telling — and to tell it in their own words.
Layer 04 · The business case
Higher margin, fewer SKUs, a brand staff actually love.
+
A niche, culturally credible brand that does the work of several lower-margin products. Dealers can't out-breadth the big-box shops — but they can carry the bike people are obsessed with.
